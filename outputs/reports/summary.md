# BioVid Part B — results summary for the paper

Generated by Step 11 of `biovid_partB_pipeline.ipynb` from the files in `outputs/`; every number below is read from those files. Accuracy is the proportion of trials classified correctly. Chance is 0.20 (5 classes), 0.25 (4 classes) and 0.50 (2 balanced classes). 95% CIs come from 2000 subject-level bootstrap resamples; p values are two-sided paired bootstrap tests on the same resamples.

- **5-class, 169 features (no facial similarity)** (86 subjects, 8426 trials, chance 0.20): XGBoost accuracy 0.360 [0.336, 0.385], macro-F1 0.354, quadratic kappa 0.451; HGB accuracy 0.351 [0.327, 0.376], macro-F1 0.346, quadratic kappa 0.431.
- **4-class BL1/PA2/PA3/PA4, 175 features** (86 subjects, 6755 trials, chance 0.25): XGBoost accuracy 0.440 [0.413, 0.468], macro-F1 0.437, quadratic kappa 0.487; HGB accuracy 0.435 [0.406, 0.464], macro-F1 0.432, quadratic kappa 0.485.

- **4-class BL1/PA2/PA3/PA4, no facial EMG** (86 subjects, 6755 trials, chance 0.25): XGBoost accuracy 0.408 [0.379, 0.439], macro-F1 0.404, quadratic kappa 0.427; HGB accuracy 0.404 [0.376, 0.432], macro-F1 0.402, quadratic kappa 0.410.

- **Self-inclusion in the BL1 template** inflates 5-class accuracy from 0.522 to 0.661 (XGBoost); removing the six facial similarity features lowers it further to 0.360.

- **Our vs the published features, no facial EMG and no similarity features, strict like for like** (84 subjects, 8226 trials, XGBoost on both): ours significantly higher in 5, lower in 0, not different in 1 of 6 tasks (ours vs published: 5-class 0.322 vs 0.289; 4-class 0.404 vs 0.355; BL1 vs PA1 0.534 vs 0.520; BL1 vs PA2 0.587 vs 0.548; BL1 vs PA3 0.651 vs 0.612; BL1 vs PA4 0.752 vs 0.708).

Their similarity features separate BL1 from pain on channels where a leave-one-out template shows no effect (Section 5.3).
- **Protocol effect**: with within-subject z-scoring, a random 75/25 trial split and LOSO give nearly the same accuracy (largest difference 0.057 over all tasks, feature sets and models). On the published features an RBF-SVM reaches 0.841 (split) and 0.842 (LOSO) for BL1 vs PA4, against the published 0.9094. Subject overlap alone therefore does not explain the published accuracies; their forward feature selection and SVM grid search were not reproduced here.

- **EDA**: statistics of the raw signal; tonic component by a 2nd-order Butterworth low-pass at 0.05 Hz (zero phase), phasic = raw - tonic; Hjorth parameters; SCR-like peaks by prominence 0.10 on the z-scored signal with 0.5 s minimum distance; Welch PSD of the phasic signal (nperseg 2048) with band powers 0.05-0.5, 0.5-1.5 and 1.5-5 Hz and spectral shape.
- **ECG**: amplitude statistics, Hjorth, Welch PSD (nperseg 1024) with bands 0.5-5, 5-15, 15-40 Hz; R peaks from a 5-20 Hz band-pass envelope (80 ms smoothing, 90th-percentile threshold raised while the rate exceeds 180 bpm, 0.3 s refractory); time-domain HRV only (5.5 s windows do not support LF/HF).
- **EMG** (each channel, after 50 and 100 Hz notch filters, Q = 30): amplitude, RMS, MAV, waveform length, zero crossings, slope sign changes, Hjorth, Welch PSD (nperseg 1024) with bands 10-30, 30-60, 60-100, 100-150 Hz and spectral shape, burst statistics over 20 RMS windows, STFT spectral flux.
- **Baseline similarity** (every channel): Pearson correlation, magnitude-squared coherence (mean, median, PSD-weighted) and binned mutual information between the trial and the subject's mean BL1 signal. The template excludes the trial itself (leave-one-out) in every reported run except `full_template_5class`.
- **Hygiene**: 0 constant features dropped, then 57 dropped by a greedy pass at |r| > 0.95 on within-subject z-scores, leaving 175.
- **Feature sets used**: 175 (all kept), 169 (without the six facial similarity features `emg_corrugator_sim_corr`, `emg_corrugator_sim_coh_mean`, `emg_corrugator_sim_mi`, `emg_zygomaticus_sim_corr`, `emg_zygomaticus_sim_coh_mean`, `emg_zygomaticus_sim_mi`), and 113 without any corrugator or zygomaticus feature.

- **Validation**: leave-one-subject-out (86 folds); every model sees identical folds.
- **Normalisation**: each feature is z-scored within subject using all of that subject's trials (labels unused); features constant within a subject are set to 0.
- **XGBoost**: objective=multi:softprob, eval_metric=mlogloss, tree_method=hist, learning_rate=0.07, max_depth=3, min_child_weight=10, subsample=0.8, colsample_bytree=0.6, reg_lambda=2.0, reg_alpha=0.0, n_estimators=300, random_state=42 (binary tasks: objective binary:logistic). Parameters come from Part A and were not tuned on Part B.
- **HistGradientBoosting (HGB)**: loss=log_loss, learning_rate=0.07, max_iter=300, max_depth=3, max_leaf_nodes=15, min_samples_leaf=20, l2_regularization=2.0, max_bins=255, early_stopping=False, random_state=42 (capacity-matched to XGBoost).
- **Logistic regression**: multinomial, L2, lbfgs, C = 1 (grid 0.01, 0.1, 1 on the main runs).
- **SVM**: RBF kernel, gamma = 'scale', one-vs-one, C = 1 (grid 0.1, 1, 10 on the main runs); AUC uses a softmax of the decision values.
- **No class weighting, no feature selection, single seed (42)**. Classes are balanced by design (20 trials per class and subject before exclusions).
- **Statistics**: accuracy, macro-F1, quadratic and unweighted Cohen's kappa, MAE in levels, Spearman rho, within-one-level rate, one-vs-rest AUC; 2000 subject-level bootstrap resamples for CIs; paired bootstrap for model and feature-set differences.

With the full template every BL1 trial is part of its own reference, so its similarity is inflated and BL1 becomes trivially separable. The leave-one-out template removes this.

### 5.2 Facial-EMG duplication between BL1 and PA1 in the release

| channel | subjects with BL1 set = PA1 set | PA1 trials identical to a BL1 trial | minimum per-subject match |
|---|---|---|---|
| emg_corrugator | 72 / 86 | 1656 / 1671 (99.1%) | 0.750 |
| emg_zygomaticus | 72 / 86 | 1656 / 1671 (99.1%) | 0.750 |

Source CSVs confirm it (`071309_w_21-BL1-081` vs `071309_w_21-PA1-001`: gsr different, ecg different, emg_trapezius different, emg_corrugator identical, emg_zygomaticus identical). In the leave-one-out 5-class run with all 175 features, the 1656 twin pairs are both classified correctly in 42.5% (XGBoost) of cases and the PA1 twin receives the higher PA1 probability in 99.5%: the facial similarity features act as a PA1 detector.

**Which class holds the copy.** Within-subject correlation between our EMG amplitude (RMS) and the published amplitude (A-RMS), which Gruss et al. computed from the original recordings:

| channel | BL1 | PA1 | PA2 | PA3 | PA4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| corrugator | 0.341 | 0.912 | 0.939 | 0.932 | 0.950 |
| zygomaticus | 0.222 | 0.950 | 0.952 | 0.956 | 0.968 |
| trapezius | 0.802 | 0.801 | 0.829 | 0.844 | 0.842 |

Facial agreement is high in PA1-PA4 and low only in BL1, while trapezius agrees in every class.

| channel | published PA1 rows identical to a BL1 row | twin r (ours) | twin r (published BL1 vs PA1 rows) |
|---|---|---|---|
| corrugator | 14 / 1680 | 1.000 | 0.308 |
| zygomaticus | 16 / 1680 | 1.000 | 0.212 |
| trapezius | 0 / 1680 | 0.424 | 0.324 |

For the released twin pairs our amplitudes are identical by construction (r = 1), while the published BL1 and PA1 rows of the same pairs correlate only 0.308 (corrugator) and 0.212 (zygomaticus), as expected for two different recordings. Conclusion: **the copied class is BL1**. The released BL1 corrugator and zygomaticus signals are PA1 recordings, while the PA1-PA4 facial signals are genuine. Consequences: (i) facial similarity features leak (above); (ii) in every run that keeps facial EMG, the BL1 facial features describe PA1 trials, so facial information about baseline vs pain is lost. The no-facial runs avoid both problems.

Largest single-feature BL1-vs-PA1 separation (|2 AUC - 1|) of the facial similarity features: ours 0.872, published 0.542.

### 5.3 Self-inclusion in the published similarity features

Largest BL1-vs-PA2..PA4 separation |2 AUC - 1| of a single similarity feature per channel (PA1 left out because of the facial copy; within-subject z-scored; matched trials):

| channel | ours, full template | ours, leave-one-out | published |
|---|---|---|---|
| corrugator | 0.884 | 0.069 | 0.557 |
| ecg | 0.901 | 0.059 | n/a |
| gsr | 0.343 | 0.261 | 0.203 |
| trapezius | 0.888 | 0.049 | 0.516 |
| zygomaticus | 0.769 | 0.093 | 0.321 |

Where our leave-one-out features show no baseline effect (ECG, trapezius), our full-template features and the published features still separate BL1 from pain. This is the signature of a baseline template that contains the trial itself, which the paper's description (similarity to the person's mean baseline signal) implies. The `nofacial_nosim` comparison therefore removes similarity features from both sets.

## 11. Limitations and points for the discussion

- Within-subject z-scoring uses all of the test subject's trials (without labels); this is standard for BioVid but is mildly optimistic compared with training-fold-only scaling, and it assumes a calibration recording per subject.
- Hyperparameters were fixed in advance (XGBoost from Part A; HGB capacity-matched; baselines at library defaults with a small C grid). No nested tuning was run.
- Results use one seed; the subject bootstrap captures between-subject variance, not seed variance.
- 5.5 s windows only allow time-domain HRV; ECG contributes little at this length.
- The released facial EMG of BL1 is a copy of PA1 (Section 5.2). Runs that keep facial EMG therefore describe PA1 faces in the BL1 class; the no-facial runs are the cleanest estimate but lose the most informative channels reported in the literature.
- Part B shares 84 of 86 subjects with Part A.
- The comparison with Gruss et al. uses their features but not their feature selection or SVM grid search; their accuracies were obtained with a random trial split and are not comparable with LOSO numbers.
- The published similarity features behave as if each BL1 trial were part of its own baseline template (Section 5.3); this cannot be confirmed without their code, so the strict comparison simply excludes all similarity features.
- The link between published rows and released trials relies on chronological order; it is supported by within-subject correlations of 0.8-0.97 on intact channels against 0.16-0.36 for a one-trial shift.
